Quick context — margin vs. day trading

Margin trading lets users borrow money from the brokerage to buy more stocks than they could with their own money. Day trading is similar, but users can only hold stocks until end of market hours — with bigger leverage. If users haven't sold by close, we force-sell at market price.

Day trading also has a much lower barrier to entry: you need around $12,200 to access margin trading, while any deposit amount unlocks day trading directly.

Chapter 1 — Product Foundations

Having leverage without a massive deposit and no loan fees sounds great — but what's the catch? The team aligned on core product fundamentals before any design work began.

Market research & concept testing

We identified that 25.7–34.2% of our user base matched the target criteria. We invited them for interviews and concept testing. While the core concept tested smoothly, users had lingering doubts:

  • Is this a loan? I don't want to use a loan to trade.
  • Why do we force-sell at end of day? I want to hold longer.
  • Does this mean I can also get a bigger loss?
Risk management reframe

We reframed force-sell as a risk management mechanism, not a downside. No fees are charged for leverage, and we were explicitly forbidden to use the word "Loan" anywhere in the product.

Chapter 2 — Haircut Value & Multiplier

Haircut value is our internal term for stock risk — higher-risk stocks reduce buying power more. The problem: this data was confidential. We couldn't explain to users why their buying power dropped.

I proposed replacing the haircut value concept with a multiplier system. Safer stocks = 5x buying power. More volatile stocks = 2x–4x. This made risk immediately readable — and turned high-multiplier stocks into desirable trading targets.

Fish tank method — gradual rollout

Like not throwing a fish directly into new tank water, we launched only with 5x on low-risk blue-chip stocks first. Users quickly asked for more volatile stocks. We expanded with no reported complaints.

Chapter 3 — Onboarding Chaos

One month after launch: only 25% of eligible users finished onboarding, but 87% of those who did used the feature daily. Root causes:

  • Onboarding information was unclear and overwhelming
  • The copy said "Forced sell" — users found it alarming
  • Unnecessary account verification steps adding friction

We iterated fast — made onboarding instant, rewrote the copy, and folded all agreements into the main T&C, eliminating verification steps for new users entirely.

Chapter 5 — Making it Even Faster

Following the successful launch, the goal was to eliminate the manual wallet transfer step — users can now access day trading from the order page directly, deposit instantly, and execute without switching wallets.

Closing

The onboarding chaos was a lesson in building fast and fixing fast. The multiplier idea was a lesson in reframing complexity into clarity.
